Reflection on this Artefact

Reflecting on this summary, I can see how my understanding of Decision Trees and PCA developed from a straightforward technical comparison into a more governance-aware and systems-level perspective through peer feedback. Initially, I framed both methods in relatively simple complementary terms: Decision Trees as interpretable supervised models and PCA as an efficient unsupervised dimensionality reduction technique. This provided a useful starting point, but it did not fully capture how these methods behave in real-world machine learning pipelines.
The peer discussions significantly deepened this view. Feedback around ensemble methods helped me recognise that interpretability in Decision Trees is not absolute, and that improved performance often comes with additional requirements for fairness evaluation and explainability tools. Similarly, clarifying PCA’s role as a statistical transformation rather than a predictive model helped me better position it within the preprocessing stage of workflows rather than as a standalone analytical approach.
A key development in my thinking was the introduction of governance-oriented perspectives, particularly the idea of “policy dials” and the influence of regulatory constraints. This shifted my focus from purely algorithmic trade-offs to broader considerations such as auditability, fairness, and accountability in system design.
Overall, this discussion helped me move from comparing individual algorithms to understanding them as interconnected components within structured, ethically governed machine learning pipelines such as the KDD process.
