Reflection on this Artefact

Why WEKA for a Revolut analysis?

WEKA's value here is not that Revolut would use it — they wouldn't. It's a controlled environment for understanding model behaviour before the complexity of production systems obscures the fundamentals. Running three algorithms on the same dataset in WEKA makes it possible to isolate the effect of algorithm choice on recall and AUC, without confounding variables from data pipelines, feature engineering, or infrastructure. The Unit 12 report will then use these controlled results as a foundation for arguing what Revolut would need to do differently at scale.

Why emphasise recall over accuracy?

In credit risk, a false negative — approving a loan that defaults — carries far greater financial cost than a false positive — rejecting a creditworthy applicant. Accuracy is misleading on imbalanced datasets: a model that predicts "good credit" for everyone scores ~70% accuracy on the German Credit Dataset but catches zero defaults. Recall on the minority class (defaults) is the metric that reflects the real cost structure. This is also why SMOTE is included in the preprocessing — to give the model a fair chance of learning the minority class pattern before we evaluate it.

What do I expect the Unit 12 findings to show?

Based on the literature, Random Forest is likely to outperform on AUC and recall, while Decision Tree (J48) will produce the most interpretable rules — potentially showing which features (duration, credit amount, employment status) are most predictive. Logistic Regression should perform reasonably as a baseline. The more interesting part will be Section 6: translating a WEKA result on 1,000 rows of German 1990s data into a recommendation for a platform processing millions of transactions in 30+ markets using GNNs and transformers.
