Reflection on this Artefact

In this summary post, I synthesised the peer contributions into a more cohesive argument, which helped me develop stronger skills in critical evaluation and thematic integration. Rather than treating each peer response separately, I worked to identify shared tensions across the discussion, particularly the balance between AI-driven economic value and ethical responsibility.
This exercise strengthened my ability to compare and contrast different perspectives. For example, I considered arguments around governance and self-regulation, organisational capability gaps, cross-sector comparisons such as healthcare, and the importance of explainability and digital literacy. I did not simply agree with these points but critically evaluated their limitations, such as the realism of user responsibility in understanding complex algorithms or the feasibility of applying healthcare-level standards to social media.
I also improved my ability to connect practical examples to broader theoretical frameworks, including general-purpose technology, ethics-by-design, and human-centred AI development. This helped me move from descriptive analysis toward more conceptual and structured academic reasoning.
Overall, this reflection demonstrated my ability to synthesise multiple viewpoints into a unified argument. It reinforced the idea that AI in social media cannot be understood through either economic or ethical lenses alone, but rather through their interdependence, particularly where trust and long-term sustainability are concerned.
