From WEKA Prototypes to Production AI

The WEKA experiments illustrate core principles that directly inform Revolut's production pipeline. SMOTE demonstrated that synthetic oversampling improves minority recall — but in production, Revolut would favour class-weighted loss functions or Focal Loss over SMOTE to avoid distribution distortion at scale.

Random Forest's superiority confirms that ensemble methods are well-suited to Revolut's heterogeneous financial data. However, their limited interpretability must be addressed with SHAP explainability layers to satisfy regulators (Hjelkrem and Lange, 2023; Hlongwane et al., 2024). The step beyond WEKA involves Graph Neural Networks for modelling borrower relationship networks, transformer embeddings for narrative text fields, and temporal cross-validation to simulate recessionary stress — all discussed in the Unit 9 report.

Full Report

1. Introduction and Business Context

The financial technology sector is entering a new stage where artificial intelligence is no longer an optional add-on but a central capability for competitiveness (Chang et al., 2024; Pasupuleti, 2024). Revolut already applies AI in fraud prevention and customer verification (Revolut, 2023). Ensemble methods such as gradient-boosted trees form part of the credit scoring pipeline, while supervised classifiers power fraud detection models (Zhou, Shen and Ballester, 2023; Valdrighi et al., 2024). This report investigates the feasibility of deploying AI for credit risk assessment using a controlled WEKA experiment, demonstrating how methodological choices affect business outcomes, and bridging from these prototypes toward advanced hybrid and graph-based approaches.

2. Dataset Selection

The German Credit Dataset from the UCI Machine Learning Repository (Hofmann, 1994) contains 1,000 loan applicants described by 20 attributes — credit history, savings, employment status, and personal characteristics — with a binary label indicating good or bad credit risk. It mirrors the structured tabular data Revolut would analyse for loan applications. The dataset's ~70/30 class imbalance (good/bad credit) reflects the real-world challenge that defaults are rarer but far more consequential. Its moderate size and well-documented nature make it ideal for methodological experimentation in WEKA, though it remains a simplified proxy for Revolut's richer behavioural and transactional features.

3. Approach

Following import and preprocessing (converting categorical attributes to nominal variables), model evaluation was performed using 10-fold cross-validation — training on nine folds and testing on one in rotation. This provides a robust generalisation estimate particularly valuable for a 1,000-record dataset (Teodorescu and Brașoveanu, 2025). SMOTE was applied within training folds only to generate synthetic minority examples, preserving evaluation integrity. Three classifiers — J48, Logistic Regression, and Random Forest — were evaluated with and without SMOTE, with emphasis on recall for the minority "bad credit" class.

4. Algorithm Selection

J48 provides interpretable decision paths for regulatory accountability. Logistic Regression offers the historical baseline of the financial industry, with coefficients directly describing feature importance (Chen and Ye, 2022; Shi et al., 2022). Random Forest, as the ensemble method, introduces greater predictive robustness at the cost of reduced interpretability — an issue addressed in production via SHAP (Hjelkrem and Lange, 2023; Hlongwane, Ramabao and Mongwe, 2024). Together, the three algorithms create a structured progression from transparent to powerful.

5. Results

5.1 J48 Decision Tree

Without SMOTE: 70.7% accuracy, Recall (bad) = 0.39, Kappa = 0.25. With SMOTE: accuracy stable at 71.2%, Recall (bad) improved to 0.52, Kappa to 0.32. SMOTE effectively balanced the learning process, improving coverage of risky profiles with minor precision loss.

5.2 Logistic Regression

Without SMOTE: 75.2% accuracy, Kappa 0.375, ROC area 0.785, Recall (bad) 0.490. With SMOTE: accuracy 72.4%, Recall (bad) 0.600, Precision 0.536, F-Measure 0.566. The model benefited from rebalancing despite reduced overall accuracy — confirming the importance of class distribution correction.

5.3 Random Forest

Without SMOTE: 76.5% accuracy, Kappa 0.37, ROC area 0.79, Recall (bad) 0.417. With SMOTE: accuracy 76.0%, Recall (bad) 0.563, Kappa 0.42, Precision 0.608, F-Measure 0.585. Random Forest retained predictive power under resampling and offered superior generalisation across all comparisons.

5.4 Comparative Analysis

Random Forest achieved the highest overall accuracy (76.5%) and F-measure (0.746) without SMOTE. Logistic Regression offered strong interpretability and stable accuracy (75.2%). SMOTE marginally improved minority recall for all models but slightly reduced overall accuracy. Random Forest maintained the best precision-recall balance; Logistic Regression offers practical interpretability for compliance contexts. J48, while easiest to explain, produced the lowest predictive power.

6. Transferability to Revolut

The WEKA experiments provide actionable insights for Revolut's credit risk pipeline. SMOTE illustrated how oversampling mitigates class imbalance, though in production Revolut would favour algorithmic approaches such as class-weighted or focal loss optimisation (Batista, Prati and Monard, 2004). Random Forest's superiority confirms ensemble methods suit Revolut's heterogeneous data, but explainability via SHAP remains essential for regulatory compliance (Hjelkrem and Lange, 2023). Under the EU AI Act, human oversight, fairness auditing, and traceable model documentation are mandatory (Veale and Zuiderveen Borgesius, 2021; European Commission, 2024).

7. Conclusion

This experiment demonstrated how machine learning can enhance Revolut's credit risk assessment by balancing predictive accuracy with regulatory transparency. Through comparative evaluation and SMOTE-based adjustment, the study highlighted the trade-offs between interpretability and performance, offering a foundation for developing scalable, compliant, and explainable credit decisioning models.

Reflection

How did the Unit 10 outline help?

The outline forced algorithm selection and metric prioritisation decisions before running a single experiment. Deciding in advance to emphasise recall over accuracy — and to justify that choice in writing — meant the analysis had a clear business interpretation from the start. Without the outline, there is a risk of selecting metrics post-hoc to make results look favourable. Pre-registering recall as the primary metric made the SMOTE improvements meaningful rather than cherry-picked.

What did the results confirm and what surprised me?

Random Forest's superiority was expected. What was more interesting was Logistic Regression's recall of 0.60 after SMOTE — the highest of any model — despite being the simplest. This is counterintuitive: the most interpretable model achieved the best minority recall after resampling. The explanation lies in the decision boundary: Logistic Regression's linear boundary shifts more dramatically in response to the rebalanced training distribution than the non-parametric Random Forest, which partially buffers the effect through ensemble averaging.

How does this connect to the Unit 9 report?

The Unit 9 report proposed GNNs, transformer embeddings, Focal Loss, and federated learning for Revolut — all sophisticated production techniques. This WEKA experiment shows why those techniques are needed: even the best WEKA model (Random Forest + SMOTE) achieves only 0.563 recall on a clean, simplified dataset. On Revolut's actual data — with millions of records, hundreds of features, cross-border complexity, and adversarial fraud — the gap between WEKA baselines and production requirements becomes the entire engineering and research agenda.
