Reflection on this Artefact

Reflecting on my discussion post, I found it valuable to compare supervised and unsupervised learning through Decision Trees and PCA, as it helped deepen my understanding of how different machine learning approaches serve distinct purposes. Researching Decision Trees highlighted their strength in interpretability, particularly in high-stakes domains such as healthcare and finance, where transparency and accountability are essential. At the same time, I became more aware of their limitations, especially overfitting and the risk of reproducing bias from training data, which reinforced the importance of data quality and ethical considerations in supervised learning.
Exploring PCA gave me a clearer understanding of how unsupervised learning can simplify complex datasets by reducing dimensionality. I initially focused on its technical benefits, such as efficiency and noise reduction, but I also recognised that reduced interpretability can create challenges, particularly when decisions become less transparent.
Overall, this comparison helped me see how these approaches complement each other in real-world applications, rather than existing in isolation. It also strengthened my awareness that choosing an algorithm is not only a technical decision but also an ethical one, requiring consideration of fairness, transparency and societal impact.

Assignment Outline

Assignment Context

Individual Essay — Due Unit 9

Leveraging Artificial Intelligence to Improve Competitiveness in a Finance Start-up

This outline was prepared in Unit 6 as a draft for tutor feedback ahead of the Unit 9 individual essay submission. The report is addressed to senior management and argues that three targeted AI applications — automated credit risk assessment, customer service chatbots, and real-time fraud detection — can help a finance start-up compete with established institutions despite limited resources.

Finance Start-up Machine Learning NLP Chatbots Fraud Detection

Three Key AI Applications

The report focuses on three areas where AI can deliver measurable competitive advantage for a start-up operating in loan provision, investment advisory, and payment services.

📊
Application 01

Automated Credit Risk Assessment

AI Technology
Machine Learning models — supervised classification trained on historical repayment data (Bhatore et al., 2020)
Data Required
Applicant demographics, financial records, repayment history, credit scores, transactional data
Business Impact
Faster loan decisions, reduced default rates, increased profitability
Key Risk
Bias in training data; lack of model transparency; regulatory compliance (Ahmed, 2024)
Case study — Zest AI: AI-driven credit scoring that expands financial inclusion while reducing default rates (Zest AI, 2024)
💬
Application 02

Customer Service Chatbots

AI Technology
Natural Language Processing (NLP) chatbots integrated with customer management systems (Doshi, 2021)
Data Required
Historical customer service records, FAQs, query logs
Business Impact
Reduced staffing costs, 24/7 availability, improved customer satisfaction
Key Risk
Query misinterpretation; weak escalation mechanisms frustrate customers
Case study — Bank of America "Erica": 2 billion interactions, serving 42 million clients since launch (Bank of America, 2024)
🔐
Application 03

Fraud Detection & Prevention

AI Technology
Anomaly detection models — unsupervised learning on transactional patterns (Hilal et al., 2021)
Data Required
Transaction histories, geolocation data, device information, behavioural patterns
Business Impact
Reduced fraud losses, enhanced trust, stronger regulatory compliance
Key Risk
False positives blocking legitimate transactions — requires careful model calibration (Hilal et al., 2021)
Case study — PayPal: anomaly detection across billions of daily transactions (PayPal, 2023)

Data Requirements & Development Approach

Application Data Required Development Approach
Credit Risk Assessment Financial history, repayment records, credit scores, demographics Supervised ML models, train-test split, cross-validation, continuous retraining
Customer Chatbots Query logs, FAQs, past service records NLP model training, intent classification, escalation workflows
Fraud Detection Transaction logs, user behaviour, location data Unsupervised anomaly detection, real-time monitoring, feedback loop to reduce false positives

Strengths & Weaknesses of AI Approaches

✓  Strengths
  • Improves decision-making speed and accuracy across all three application areas
  • Enables automation, substantially reducing operational costs for a resource-constrained start-up
  • Scales well as the business grows — a key advantage over human-only workflows (Milana and Ashta, 2021)
!  Weaknesses
  • Risk of bias and lack of explainability — particularly acute in credit scoring decisions (Cao, 2023)
  • Dependence on high-quality, large-scale data — difficult for a start-up without historical records
  • Requires continuous monitoring and retraining to remain accurate as market conditions shift

Implementation Challenges

⚖️

Ethical & Bias Concerns

Historical data may reflect past discrimination, leading to biased outcomes in credit scoring — requiring diverse training data and regular bias audits (Ahmed, 2024).

🛡️

Data Privacy & Security

Handling sensitive financial and personal data requires full GDPR compliance and robust data governance frameworks (Adegbite, 2025).

🔧

Technical Expertise

Developing and maintaining ML models requires skilled personnel and infrastructure investment that may strain a start-up's budget (Amrit and Narayanappa, 2024).

🤝

Customer Trust

Customers may feel uneasy about AI-driven decisions — particularly automated loan rejections — unless transparency and human oversight are clearly demonstrated (DeZao, 2024).

Reflection on this Artefact

Why these three AI applications?

Credit risk, customer service, and fraud detection were chosen because they map directly to the core functions of a finance start-up — lending, client interaction, and transaction integrity. Each also represents a different AI paradigm (supervised ML, NLP, and unsupervised anomaly detection), which allows the essay to demonstrate breadth of technical understanding rather than repeating the same approach three times.

What did drafting the outline reveal?

Structuring the outline exposed a tension I had not initially anticipated: the strongest case studies (Zest AI, Erica, PayPal) are all from large, well-resourced organisations. A start-up lacks the historical data volumes and infrastructure these companies had access to. The final essay will need to explicitly address this gap — proposing how a start-up might use third-party ML APIs, synthetic data generation, or partnership models to overcome the cold-start problem.

What feedback did I expect from my tutor?

The outline's weakest section at the draft stage was the implementation challenges — particularly around regulatory compliance. GDPR is mentioned but not developed: which specific articles apply to automated decision-making in credit scoring (Article 22), what the right to explanation means in practice, and how FINTRAC or PSD2 might affect the payment services arm. The final essay will expand this section significantly with jurisdiction-specific regulatory analysis.
