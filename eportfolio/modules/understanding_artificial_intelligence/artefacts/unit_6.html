Reflection on this Artefact

Reflecting on my discussion post, I found it valuable to compare supervised and unsupervised learning through Decision Trees and PCA, as it helped deepen my understanding of how different machine learning approaches serve distinct purposes. Researching Decision Trees highlighted their strength in interpretability, particularly in high-stakes domains such as healthcare and finance, where transparency and accountability are essential. At the same time, I became more aware of their limitations, especially overfitting and the risk of reproducing bias from training data, which reinforced the importance of data quality and ethical considerations in supervised learning.
Exploring PCA gave me a clearer understanding of how unsupervised learning can simplify complex datasets by reducing dimensionality. I initially focused on its technical benefits, such as efficiency and noise reduction, but I also recognised that reduced interpretability can create challenges, particularly when decisions become less transparent.
Overall, this comparison helped me see how these approaches complement each other in real-world applications, rather than existing in isolation. It also strengthened my awareness that choosing an algorithm is not only a technical decision but also an ethical one, requiring consideration of fairness, transparency and societal impact.
