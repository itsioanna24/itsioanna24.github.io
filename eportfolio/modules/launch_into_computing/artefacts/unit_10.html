Discussion Forum

Reflection on this Artefact

This post shows a strong understanding of how IT governance frameworks support organisational strategy, using Toyota as a practical example. A key strength is the clear explanation of how ITIL 4 and COBIT 2019 complement each other, linking service delivery with governance, risk, and compliance. The discussion of digital transformation is also effective, particularly in highlighting the shift toward data-driven decision-making and operational efficiency.
However, the post is largely descriptive and could benefit from deeper critical evaluation. For instance, while ITIL and COBIT are presented positively, their limitations—such as implementation complexity, cost, or organisational resistance—are only briefly touched upon. A more critical comparison with alternative frameworks or approaches would strengthen the analysis.
The inclusion of challenges, such as resistance to change and legacy system integration, adds valuable depth. However, these points could be further developed by assessing how successful the mitigation strategies were, rather than simply describing them.
From a learning perspective, this reflection reinforced the importance of aligning IT strategy with broader business and sustainability goals. In future work, I will focus more on critical analysis, including evaluating trade-offs and the real impact of strategic IT decisions.

ITIL 4 in a Healthcare Organisation

An analysis of how the ITIL 4 framework can align IT with clinical objectives, mitigate service risks, and optimise resources in a healthcare provider — illustrated through the Sutter Health case study.

Governance Report

The Framework

ITIL
Information Technology Infrastructure Library — Version 4
A globally adopted IT Service Management (ITSM) framework that aligns IT services with organisational objectives through seven guiding principles, standardised practices, and a service value system. ITIL 4 introduced a more holistic, flexible structure suited to modern agile and DevOps environments.

Legal & Compliance Considerations

Regulation

GDPR

Mandates data minimisation, purpose limitation, and lawful processing of student data. Article 33 requires breach notification to the supervisory authority within 72 hours. All data handling is aligned with accountability and transparency principles (GDPR, 2016).

Ethical Framework

Ethical Design

The platform prioritises user autonomy, informed consent, and continuity of digital learning — aligned with ethical design principles for higher education learning technologies (Lachheb et al., 2023).

Framework

ISO/IEC 27001

Risk-based information security management with regular audits, access control reviews, and incident management procedures reinforces both compliance and operational resilience (ISO, 2022).

ITIL 4 — Seven Guiding Principles

These principles guide decision-making across all organisational levels, promoting a cultural shift that empowers staff to act without constant oversight (Maes, 2021).

P01

Focus on Value

Every activity should link to the value delivered to stakeholders — in healthcare, this means patient outcomes.

P02

Start Where You Are

Assess existing capabilities before implementing changes — avoiding unnecessary disruption to live clinical systems.

P03

Progress Iteratively

Implement in feedback-driven cycles, allowing healthcare IT teams to refine processes incrementally.

P04

Collaborate & Promote Visibility

Break silos between IT and clinical teams — essential for coordinating around EHR and telemedicine systems.

P05

Think & Work Holistically

Consider the full service system — changes to one component (e.g., patient portal) affect others (e.g., scheduling).

P06

Keep It Simple

Avoid unnecessary complexity in processes — critical in clinical environments where staff adoption must be fast.

P07

Optimise & Automate

Streamline repeatable processes — automating routine IT tasks frees clinical staff and reduces human error in critical workflows.

Three Strategic Objectives

The assignment asks how a governance framework achieves three goals. Here is how ITIL 4 addresses each within a healthcare context.

🎯
Objective 01 · Alignment

Align IT with Business Goals

ITIL's service design practices map IT capabilities directly to clinical objectives — Electronic Health Records (EHR), telemedicine, and patient data systems — ensuring IT investment is evaluated by its contribution to care delivery, not technical metrics alone (Hammad et al., 2024). The Service Value Chain links every IT activity back to a measurable outcome for the organisation.

🛡️
Objective 02 · Risk

Mitigate IT-Related Risks

Incident Management, Change Control, and Problem Management practices reduce the probability and impact of IT failures. ITIL also embeds GDPR compliance into service design, reducing legal risk from data breaches. Standardised change procedures prevent unplanned outages from rushed updates — a common failure mode in healthcare IT (Al-Ashmoery et al., 2021).

⚙️
Objective 03 · Efficiency

Optimise IT Resources

ITIL's Optimise and Automate principle and its focus on continual service improvement reduce operational costs through process standardisation. In healthcare, this translates to faster resolution of technical incidents — minimising clinician downtime — and scalable infrastructure for demand surges such as public health emergencies (Elwell, 2024).

Implementation Strategy

Implementation in a healthcare setting should follow a phased approach, starting with high-impact, lower-disruption areas before expanding to organisation-wide service management.

Phase 01

Capability Assessment

Evaluate existing ITSM maturity — identify gaps in incident logging, change tracking, and service desk functions.

Phase 02

Core Practices First

Adopt Incident Management, Change Control, and Service Desk as initial focus areas — matching the Sutter Health phased approach (Axelos, 2017).

Phase 03

Clinical Alignment

Map ITIL practices to specific clinical systems — EHR, telemedicine, patient portals — ensuring IT outcomes link to care delivery metrics.

Phase 04

Training & Improvement

Embed ITIL principles via staff training, then monitor KPIs and user feedback to drive continual service improvement cycles.

Benefits & Challenges

✓  Benefits in Healthcare
  • Faster resolution of technical issues affecting critical systems like EHR — reducing clinician downtime
  • Improved coordination between IT and clinical teams through shared service vocabulary and processes
  • GDPR compliance embedded into service design, reducing legal exposure from patient data breaches
  • Scalable infrastructure for demand surges — public health emergencies, seasonal peaks, or rapid expansion
  • Consistent service performance supporting better, more reliable patient outcomes
!  Challenges to Address
  • Cultural resistance from clinical and IT staff unfamiliar with structured, process-driven service management
  • Scope management — attempting full ITIL adoption at once risks overwhelming the organisation
  • Sustained leadership commitment required; ITIL implementation stalls without executive sponsorship
  • Training investment is significant — particularly in large healthcare organisations with varied digital literacy
🏥
Real-World Case Study

Sutter Health — Phased ITIL Adoption

Sutter Health's ITIL implementation illustrates both the difficulty and the value of the framework in a complex healthcare setting. The organisation faced significant cultural resistance — shifting towards a process-driven, service-oriented mindset required sustained training and active leadership support. Crucially, Sutter Health avoided implementation overload by adopting a phased approach: beginning with high-impact areas such as Incident and Problem Management before expanding further. This sequencing is now widely cited as a best-practice model for healthcare ITIL rollouts (Axelos, 2017).

Full Report

Introduction

The Information Technology Infrastructure Library (ITIL) is a widely adopted approach to IT Service Management (ITSM) that aims to ensure IT services effectively support organisational objectives. For a healthcare provider, where system reliability and service quality are critical, ITIL offers a practical approach to managing IT operations effectively and securely.

Overview

This Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP) is proposed for a cloud-based Learning Management System (LMS) designed to support academic institutions in delivering online education, enabling course management, assignment submission, student data storage, and virtual assessments. The platform is fully hosted on cloud infrastructure, primarily relying on Amazon Web Services (AWS) for computing, storage, and database management.

Identified Risks and Impacts

The platform faces several potential risks that could impact its service delivery. Cloud service outages can occur due to regional failures in cloud provider infrastructure (Cotroneo et al., 2019), making the platform temporarily inaccessible and disrupting teaching and learning activities. Cybersecurity risks encompass DDoS attacks, unauthorised data access, and ransomware infections, potentially leading to data exposure, service downtime, and legal liabilities (Lallie et al., 2023). System failures caused by software bugs, failed updates, or corrupted databases can impair core functionality. Additionally, authentication failures may lock users out during assessments, while insider threats can result in data loss or intentional compromise.

Key Principles of ITIL 4

According to Maes (2021), ITIL 4 has seven guiding principles: Focus on value, Start where you are, Progress iteratively with feedback, Collaborate and promote visibility, Think and work holistically, Keep it simple and practical, and Optimise and automate. These are designed to guide decision-making and actions across all levels of organisational service management, and promote a cultural change that empowers people to act without constant oversight.

Disaster Recovery Strategy & Implementation

The platform's DRP is designed for rapid service restoration and risk mitigation. Platform components are deployed across multiple AWS regions and availability zones, ensuring high availability and redundancy (Berenberg and Calder, 2021).

The ITIL framework assists businesses in managing risk, building sustainable relationships with customers, maintaining low-cost operations, and supporting a dynamic infrastructure that ensures flexibility and global scalability (Al-Ashmoery et al., 2021). In a healthcare environment — where service continuity, data protection, and responsiveness are critical — ITIL offers a practical framework to manage IT services efficiently. By promoting standardised processes, ITIL helps reduce risks, control operational costs, and support scalable infrastructure.

The step-by-step recovery process proceeds as follows:

  1. Automated alerts detect abnormal activity or system failures.
  2. Immediate isolation of affected systems prevents escalation.
  3. Failover systems, prepared using AWS Elastic Disaster Recovery, are activated to shift operations to a secondary region.
  4. The latest encrypted backup is restored using Bacula, chosen for its ransomware-resistant capabilities and support for the 3-2-1 backup strategy (Sheriffdeen & Ade, 2023; Bacula Systems, 2024).
  5. Data integrity checks are performed.
  6. Critical functions — logins, file uploads, and assessments — are tested to confirm system stability.
  7. Users are notified via email and system status pages.
  8. A post-incident review identifies root causes and documentation is updated accordingly.

Benefits

The ITIL framework improves organisational efficiency, service quality, and business-IT alignment by standardising processes and enabling greater flexibility (Elwell, 2024). In a healthcare environment, these benefits translate into faster resolution of technical issues affecting critical systems like EHR, and improved coordination between IT and clinical teams. It also supports regulatory compliance — particularly GDPR — by embedding compliance into service design and delivery processes.

Tools and Technologies

The platform leverages AWS CloudTrail and GuardDuty for real-time monitoring and threat detection (Oye and Clark, 2021); Bacula for encrypted, flexible backups with geographically redundant storage; and Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) with strict access controls to reduce unauthorised access risk (Wang et al., 2024).

Legal, Ethical, and Professional Considerations

The platform operates under strict legal and ethical obligations for student data. It complies fully with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), with particular attention to data minimisation, purpose limitation, and lawful processing (Aikaterini Daoultzoglou, 2023). If a data breach occurs, the platform complies with GDPR Article 33 by reporting the incident within 72 hours (GDPR, 2016). All user data is encrypted, and data handling practices are aligned with accountability and transparency principles.

Ethically, the platform prioritises user autonomy, informed consent, and continuity of digital learning, aligning with ethical design principles for educational technologies (Lachheb et al., 2023). Regular audits, recovery drills, and staff training reinforce both compliance and resilience.

Challenges

Despite its benefits, implementing ITIL in complex environments can present challenges, particularly around organisational culture and adoption of structured processes. Resistance to change and the need for phased implementation can slow deployment, requiring strong leadership and staff training (Axelos, 2017).

Conclusion

This combined ITIL-driven DRP ensures that the platform remains resilient, secure, and compliant while delivering uninterrupted digital education. By integrating structured governance practices with a multi-layered disaster recovery strategy, the system is positioned to minimise downtime, reduce risk, and maintain service continuity across all failure scenarios.

Reflection

Why ITIL over COBIT or TOGAF?

The choice of ITIL was deliberate. COBIT is primarily a control and audit framework — better suited to governance oversight than day-to-day service delivery. TOGAF is an architectural framework, focused on enterprise design rather than operational service management. For a healthcare organisation where the immediate challenge is reliable, compliant service delivery — not enterprise architecture or audit governance — ITIL's operational focus and practical practice library was the most directly applicable choice.

What did the Sutter Health case add?

The Sutter Health example transformed the analysis from theoretical to evidential. The two specific lessons — that cultural resistance is the primary implementation risk, and that phased adoption starting with Incident Management is the recommended approach — gave the report concrete practical grounding. It also made me reconsider the relationship between framework adoption and change management: ITIL is as much a cultural shift as a technical one.

How did healthcare change the analysis?

Applying ITIL to healthcare — rather than a generic enterprise — revealed tensions I would not have otherwise considered. In most industries, IT downtime means lost revenue. In healthcare, it means delayed diagnoses or inaccessible patient records during clinical emergencies. This raised the stakes of every implementation decision, particularly around Change Control: the risk of a failed update taking down an EHR system during active patient care is qualitatively different from the same failure in a retail context.

What would I extend in future work?

I would develop a maturity assessment against the ITIL 4 Service Value System to produce a gap analysis for the hypothetical healthcare organisation — identifying which of the four dimensions (organisations and people, information and technology, partners and suppliers, value streams and processes) requires the most investment. I would also explore how ITIL and COBIT can be used in combination: ITIL for service delivery, COBIT for governance assurance — a pairing recommended by several enterprise IT governance practitioners.
