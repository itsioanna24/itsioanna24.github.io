The Scenario

Cloud-Based Learning Management System (LMS)

Academic Institutions AWS Infrastructure Student Data & Assessments

A cloud-based LMS designed to support academic institutions in delivering online education — enabling course management, assignment submission, student data storage, and virtual assessments. The platform is fully hosted on AWS infrastructure for computing, storage, and database management, creating a concentrated cloud dependency that requires a robust and tested disaster recovery strategy.

Risk Categories

Identified Risks & Impacts

Five distinct risk categories were identified, spanning infrastructure availability, cybersecurity, software reliability, access control, and internal threats.

🌩️
Critical · Infrastructure

Cloud Service Outages

Regional failures in cloud provider infrastructure can render the platform temporarily inaccessible, disrupting teaching and learning activities at scale. AWS regional dependencies create single points of failure if multi-region redundancy is absent (Cotroneo et al., 2019).

🦠
Critical · Cybersecurity

DDoS, Ransomware & Unauthorised Access

Cybersecurity threats encompassing DDoS attacks, ransomware infections, and unauthorised data access can cause data exposure, service downtime, and legal liabilities. Educational platforms are increasingly targeted due to the sensitivity of student data (Lallie et al., 2023).

🐛
High · Software

System Failures

Software bugs, failed updates, or corrupted databases can impair core platform functionality — affecting course access, assignment submission, and automated grading during critical academic periods.

🔑
Medium · Access Control

Authentication Failures

System-wide authentication failures can lock students, staff, and administrators out of the platform during assessments or submission deadlines — with significant academic and reputational consequences.

🕵️
Medium · Internal

Insider Threats

Malicious or negligent internal actors — staff or administrators with elevated privileges — can cause data loss or intentional compromise of student records, equiring role-based access controls and audit logging as countermeasures.

Step-by-Step Recovery Process

The DRP is structured as an eight-stage process, from automated detection through to post-incident review and documentation.

01

Automated Detection

AWS CloudTrail and GuardDuty detect abnormal activity or system failures in real time, triggering automated alerts to the incident response team.

02

Isolation of Affected Systems

Immediate isolation of compromised or failing components prevents escalation and limits lateral spread — critical for containing ransomware.

03

Failover Activation

AWS Elastic Disaster Recovery shifts operations to a secondary AWS region, activating pre-configured failover systems to restore service availability (Berenberg and Calder, 2021).

04

Backup Restoration via Bacula

The latest encrypted backup is restored using Bacula — chosen for its ransomware-resistant capabilities and native support for the 3-2-1 backup strategy with geographically redundant storage (Sheriffdeen & Ade, 2023; Bacula Systems, 2024).

05

Data Integrity Verification

Automated hash-based integrity checks confirm that restored data is complete and unmodified — ensuring academic records and submission data are accurate before systems are brought back online.

06

Critical Function Testing

Logins, file uploads, and assessment submissions are tested across user roles to confirm system stability and correctness before full service restoration.

07

User Notification

Affected users — students, staff, and administrators — are notified via email and system status pages with service restoration timelines and any data implications requiring disclosure under GDPR.

08

Post-Incident Review

Root causes are identified, DRP documentation is updated, and staff training or recovery drill schedules are revised to prevent recurrence.

Tools & Technologies

The disaster recovery strategy relies on multiple technologies working together to ensure resilience, redundancy, monitoring, and secure recovery capabilities.

☁️

AWS Elastic Disaster Recovery

Enables rapid failover to a secondary AWS region with minimal data loss. Multi-region deployment ensures high availability and redundancy during infrastructure failures (Berenberg and Calder, 2021).

💾

Bacula Backup System

Open-source enterprise backup tool providing encrypted, flexible backups with geographically redundant storage. Natively supports the 3-2-1 strategy and is specifically designed for ransomware resistance (Bacula Systems, 2024).

🔍

AWS CloudTrail & GuardDuty

Real-time monitoring and AI-enhanced threat detection across the AWS environment. CloudTrail logs all API activity; GuardDuty identifies suspicious patterns using machine learning (Oye and Clark, 2021).

🔐

MFA & Access Controls

Multi-Factor Authentication and strict role-based access controls reduce the risk of unauthorised access — a critical defence against both external attacks and insider threats (Wang et al., 2024).

🔒

End-to-End Encryption

All user data encrypted at rest and in transit. Ensures that even in the event of a breach, intercepted data is unreadable — a GDPR accountability requirement (GDPR, 2016).

🔄

Recovery Drills & Audits

Regular DRP rehearsals and compliance audits reinforce both operational resilience and regulatory readiness — ensuring the plan remains current and staff-tested (ISO, 2022).

Legal & Compliance Considerations

Regulation

GDPR

Mandates data minimisation, purpose limitation, and lawful processing of student data. Article 33 requires breach notification to the supervisory authority within 72 hours. All data handling is aligned with accountability and transparency principles (GDPR, 2016).

Ethical Framework

Ethical Design

The platform prioritises user autonomy, informed consent, and continuity of digital learning — aligned with ethical design principles for higher education learning technologies (Lachheb et al., 2023).

Framework

ISO/IEC 27001

Risk-based information security management with regular audits, access control reviews, and incident management procedures reinforces both compliance and operational resilience (ISO, 2022).

Full Report

Overview

This Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP) is proposed for a cloud-based Learning Management System (LMS) designed to support academic institutions in delivering online education, enabling course management, assignment submission, student data storage, and virtual assessments. The platform is fully hosted on cloud infrastructure, primarily relying on Amazon Web Services (AWS) for computing, storage, and database management.

Identified Risks and Impacts

The platform faces several potential risks that could impact its service delivery. Cloud service outages can occur due to regional failures in cloud provider infrastructure (Cotroneo et al., 2019), making the platform temporarily inaccessible and disrupting teaching and learning activities. Cybersecurity risks encompass DDoS attacks, unauthorised data access, and ransomware infections, potentially leading to data exposure, service downtime, and legal liabilities (Lallie et al., 2023). System failures caused by software bugs, failed updates, or corrupted databases can impair core functionality. Additionally, authentication failures may lock users out during assessments, while insider threats can result in data loss or intentional compromise.

Disaster Recovery Strategy

The platform's DRP is designed for rapid service restoration and risk mitigation. Platform components are deployed across multiple AWS regions and availability zones, ensuring high availability and redundancy (Berenberg and Calder, 2021).

The step-by-step recovery process proceeds as follows:

  1. Automated alerts detect abnormal activity or system failures.
  2. Immediate isolation of affected systems prevents escalation.
  3. Failover systems, prepared using AWS Elastic Disaster Recovery, are activated to shift operations to a secondary region.
  4. The latest encrypted backup is restored using Bacula, chosen for its ransomware-resistant capabilities and support for the 3-2-1 backup strategy (Sheriffdeen & Ade, 2023; Bacula Systems, 2024).
  5. Data integrity checks are performed.
  6. Critical functions — logins, file uploads, and assessments — are tested to confirm system stability.
  7. Users are notified via email and system status pages.
  8. A post-incident review identifies root causes and documentation is updated accordingly.

Tools and Technologies

The platform leverages AWS CloudTrail and GuardDuty for real-time monitoring and threat detection (Oye and Clark, 2021); Bacula for encrypted, flexible backups with geographically redundant storage; and Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) with strict access controls to reduce unauthorised access risk (Wang et al., 2024).

Legal, Ethical, and Professional Considerations

The platform operates under strict legal and ethical obligations for student data. It complies fully with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), with particular attention to data minimisation, purpose limitation, and lawful processing (Aikaterini Daoultzoglou, 2023). If a data breach occurs, the platform complies with GDPR Article 33 by reporting the incident to the relevant supervisory authority within 72 hours of detection (GDPR, 2016). All user data is encrypted, and data handling practices are aligned with GDPR's accountability and transparency principles.

Ethically, the platform prioritises user autonomy, informed consent, and the continuity of digital learning, aligning with current research on ethical design in learning technologies (Lachheb et al., 2023). Regular audits, recovery drills, and staff training reinforce both compliance and resilience.

Conclusion

This DRP ensures that the platform remains resilient, secure, and compliant while delivering uninterrupted digital education. By combining multi-region AWS redundancy, Bacula's ransomware-resistant backups, real-time threat detection, and a structured eight-step recovery process, the LMS is positioned to minimise downtime and protect student data across all failure scenarios.

Reflection

What did the LMS context add to the DRP?

Choosing an educational platform as the scenario made the stakes concrete in a way a generic e-commerce example would not. Student assessments, submission deadlines, and academic records are time-sensitive and legally protected — meaning downtime has a direct and measurable harm. This framing pushed me to think beyond uptime percentages and consider what specific user journeys must be prioritised during a partial recovery.

Why Bacula over other backup tools?

Researching backup solutions revealed that Bacula's combination of native 3-2-1 strategy support, encryption, and explicit ransomware resistance made it a strong fit for a platform where data integrity is a GDPR obligation, not just an operational preference. The distinction between a tool that can be configured for ransomware resistance and one designed with it as a core feature was a meaningful selection criterion I had not previously considered.

How did GDPR shape the recovery plan?

The 72-hour breach notification window in GDPR Article 33 is not just a compliance requirement — it is a design constraint. The DRP's detection, isolation, and notification steps had to be sequenced so that a breach could be confirmed and reported within that window even under degraded conditions. This made me think of legal compliance as an input to operational design, not a separate checklist appended afterwards.

What would I extend in future work?

I would develop quantitative Recovery Time Objectives (RTOs) and Recovery Point Objectives (RPOs) for each system component, as these are the measurable targets that make a DRP testable and auditable. I would also design a formal tabletop exercise protocol — a simulated incident walkthrough — to validate the eight-step process with actual staff before a real disruption occurs.
